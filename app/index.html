💡 The 1% Rule

If you get 1% better each day, you'll be 37x better in one year.

If you get 1% worse each day, you'll decline to nearly zero.

"Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement."
The 4 Laws of Behavior Change
1️⃣ Make It Obvious
  • Implementation intention: "I will [exercise] at [6 AM] in [my room]"
  • Habit stacking: "After I [brush my teeth], I will [do 5 push-ups]"
  • Environment design: Put your workout clothes next to your bed
  • Pointing & calling: Say out loud: "It's 6 AM, time to train"
🎯 Your environment is more powerful than your motivation. Design it.
2️⃣ Make It Attractive
  • Temptation bundling: "After I complete my workout, I'll watch my show"
  • Join a tribe: Surround yourself with people who exercise daily
  • Reframe mindset: Not "I have to work out" → "I GET to build my body"
  • Motivation ritual: Play your hype song before every workout
🧲 The more attractive a habit, the more likely it becomes automatic.
3️⃣ Make It Easy
  • 2-Minute Rule: "Start with just 2 minutes" — scale down until it's effortless
  • Reduce friction: Sleep in workout clothes. Keep weights visible.
  • Prime your environment: Set up your mat, bands, and water the night before
  • Automate: Same time, same place, same routine. No decisions needed.
⚡ The most effective form of learning is practice, not planning.
4️⃣ Make It Satisfying
  • Habit tracking: Mark the X. Never break the chain. (This app does this!)
  • Immediate reward: After working out → enjoy a protein shake/smoothie
  • Never miss twice: Bad day? Do just 1 set. Show up. That's the real habit.
  • Accountability partner: Share your garden/streak with someone
✅ What is immediately rewarded is repeated. What is immediately punished is avoided.

⏱️ The Two-Minute Rule

Scale ANY habit down to 2 minutes. Then do it EVERY DAY:

  • Want to read daily? Just read 2 minutes.
  • Want to meditate? Just 2 minutes. Every day.
  • Want to go to gym? Just go ONCE a week. (1x/week = 52 times/year >>> 0)
  • Guitar? 5-10 min daily. In 1 year = amazing skills.

After 1 month: 2 min → 5 min easily. After 2 months: 5 → 10 min. It SCALES.

"LESS TIME + CONSISTENT + SUSTAINABLE > Long sessions that you'll skip. 10-15 min gym is ENOUGH sometimes."

📱 In this app: The 9-min timer IS the two-minute rule. Short enough to never skip, long enough to count.

System Design Toolkit
📋 Implementation Intention

Decide in advance: "I will [EXERCISE] at [TIME] in [PLACE]"

  • "I will do dumbbell curls at 7:00 AM in my room"
  • "I will do push-ups at 6:45 PM on the gym mat"
  • "I will stretch at 10:30 PM in the bedroom"

Think through WHEN, WHERE, WHAT, HOW, HOW MUCH — all decided beforehand. No willpower needed in the moment.

💡 Vague: "I'll work out tomorrow." → Specific: "I'll do 9-min dumbbells at 7 AM in my room." The specific one happens.

📱 In this app: The Routine tab = your implementation intention saved permanently. No deciding in the moment.

🔗 Habit Stacking

"After I [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]"

  • "After I wake up and brush, I will pick up dumbbells"
  • "After I make coffee, I will do 10 push-ups while it brews"
  • "After I close my laptop for the day, I will do a 9-min session"
  • "After I put on my playlist, I will step on the mat"

Chain habits together so one triggers the next. Morning routine becomes autopilot.

📱 In this app: "After I wake up → open FitTimer → press START" becomes your anchor stack.
